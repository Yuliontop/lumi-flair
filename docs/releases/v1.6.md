# Lumi Flair v1.6 — Audio Visualizer 🎵

Bars that move to your music, anywhere in Lumiverse. And in the desktop app, they can dance to **whatever your computer is playing**.

## 🎵 Audio visualizer
Find it in the Flair panel under **Audio visualizer** (off by default). Show it in one place or all three at once:

- 🪟 **A floating window** you can drag anywhere and resize from its corner
- 🌄 **A background layer** across the whole chat view, above the wallpaper and below the messages, with its own opacity and height
- ↔️ **A strip on each side of the chat column**

Make it yours:
- 📊 **Four styles:** bars, mirrored bars, segments, wave
- 🪞 **Center Mirror:** lows in the middle, highs mirrored out to both sides (high ← low → high), or flip it so the highs meet in the middle
- 🎨 **Colours follow your Flair Pack**, or pick two of your own, or go rainbow
- ✨ Bar count and width, rounded corners, glow, peak caps, how fast bars fall

## 🔊 What it listens to
- **Flair's own sound:** soundscapes and your "Always play" track
- **This computer's sound** ⭐ *new:* whatever your computer is playing (Spotify, YouTube, a game…) in the **Lumiverse desktop app**. See the setup guide below.
- **A tab or screen** you share with its sound (Chrome and Edge)
- **The microphone:** hears your speakers
- **A demo signal** for setting the look up in silence

It's desktop only. Phones and tablets stay clutter-free: nothing is drawn and the panel section is just a note.

## 🖥️ Setting up "This computer's sound"
**You need:** the Lumiverse **desktop app** (not a browser tab), up to date, on **Windows 10 or later**, **macOS 14.2 or later**, or **Linux with PulseAudio or PipeWire**. This works because of new system-audio support in Lumiverse itself (Lumiverse PRs #445 and #446), so older desktop builds can't do it.

**Step by step**
1. **Update Lumiverse** and make sure the desktop app is current. In the tray menu, use **Check for Updates / Apply Update**. If the app tells you it needs rebuilding, or the next steps don't show the new tray item, choose **Rebuild Desktop App…** and let it restart. (Building from source? After pulling, also run `bun run build:frontend`, because the page the app shows is built separately.)
2. **Update Lumi Flair** to 1.6: Extensions → Lumi Flair → **Update**, then **Ctrl+Shift+R**.
3. **Switch it on in the desktop app.** Open the **tray menu** (the Lumiverse icon in your system tray) and tick **Let Extensions Analyse System Audio**. Confirm with **Allow**. This is a master switch that is off by default and remembered. Nothing can hear your computer's sound until you do this.
4. **Pick the source in Lumi Flair.** Open the Flair panel → **Audio visualizer**, turn on **Show the visualizer**, and set **Listen to** to **This computer's sound**.
5. **Press Connect audio** (or **Connect** in the visualizer window).
6. **Say yes twice.** Lumiverse first asks whether to give Lumi Flair the **system audio** permission, then shows an **"Analyse system audio?"** prompt. Allow both. It asks again every time you connect.
7. **Play something.** The bars follow your music. Pause it and they settle and rest.

While it's listening, Lumiverse shows a **"Lumi Flair is analysing system audio"** pill with a **Stop** button, and the tray menu says **Extension Is Analysing System Audio**. Press Stop, or untick the tray item, to end it at once. If the pill is in the way, press its **–** button to shrink it to a small ♪ chip (click the chip to bring it back). It never disappears completely while something is listening.

**macOS:** the first time, macOS asks to allow **System Audio Recording**. It's not Screen Recording. If you said no, turn it on in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen & System Audio Recording → **System Audio Recording Only**, then press Connect again.

**Linux:** it needs the `parec` program (it's how Lumiverse reads the monitor of your speakers; the microphone is never used). Install it with `sudo apt install pulseaudio-utils` (Debian/Ubuntu), `sudo dnf install pulseaudio-utils` (Fedora) or `sudo pacman -S libpulse` (Arch). On PipeWire systems, `pipewire-pulse` provides it too. Flatpak and Snap builds don't include it, so the option reports as unavailable there.

**If it won't connect**
- *"System audio needs the Lumiverse desktop app…"*: you're in a browser tab, or the desktop app or its page is out of date. Redo step 1 (and the `bun run build:frontend` note), then reload with Ctrl+Shift+R.
- *"Tick 'Let Extensions Analyse System Audio' in the Lumiverse tray menu first"*: do step 3.
- *"Permission wasn't given"*: you chose not to allow one of the two prompts in step 6. Press Connect again.
- *Connected but the bars are flat:* check something is actually playing, that the Sensitivity slider isn't at the bottom, and (macOS) that System Audio Recording is allowed.

## 🔒 Your privacy
Lumi Flair only ever receives **a loudness level and a spectrum** (how much bass, mids and treble). It never gets the sound itself, and nothing is recorded, saved or sent anywhere. Lumiverse asks you each time, shows when it's listening, and you can stop it from the pill or the tray at any time.

## 💡 Good to know
- It stays off when "reduce motion" is on, and "No flashing" softens how fast bars jump and turns the glow off.
- It's kind to your machine: the visualizer rests completely in silence, and the desktop app only listens while sound plays and its window is visible.
- Lumi Flair asks for the new `system_audio` permission only when you press Connect with this source chosen.
- This computer's sound on **macOS and Linux is brand new**. Windows has had the most testing, so if something's off, tell us.

**Update:** click **Update** on Lumi Flair in Extensions, then refresh (Ctrl+Shift+R).
